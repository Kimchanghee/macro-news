## Three rates, one corridor

The ECB's Governing Council sets three rates that form a corridor for overnight money in the euro area:

| Rate | What it is | Level on our snapshot |
|---|---|---|
| Marginal lending facility (MLF) | What banks pay to borrow overnight from the Eurosystem against collateral; the ceiling | 2.90% |
| Main refinancing operations (MRO) | Fixed rate on the ECB's regular one-week loans | 2.65% |
| Deposit facility rate (DFR) | What banks earn on overnight deposits at the Eurosystem; the floor | 2.50% |

All three took effect on **16 September 2026** according to the ECB Data Portal series in our committed snapshot. The live levels and full history are on the [ECB interest rates page](/indicators/ecb-interest-rates/).

## Why the deposit rate is the one that matters now

Before 2008, banks borrowed from the ECB every week and the MRO rate set the tone. After years of crisis lending and asset purchases, the banking system holds far more reserves than it needs. When banks have surplus cash, the marginal euro is parked at the deposit facility, so the **deposit facility rate becomes the effective floor** under money-market rates such as €STR.

In March 2024 the ECB confirmed it would keep steering policy through the deposit facility rate and narrowed the spread between the MRO and DFR from 50 to **15 basis points**, effective 18 September 2024, to make borrowing from the ECB cheaper as excess liquidity declines. The levels above fit that design: 2.65% − 2.50% = 0.15 point, and the MLF sits 25 basis points above the MRO.

## A short history in the data

Our snapshot holds every change in the deposit rate since 2019:

- **−0.50%** from September 2019, the bottom of the negative-rate era.
- **0.00%** in July 2022, the first hike in eleven years, ending negative rates.
- **4.00%** in September 2023, the peak of the tightening cycle.
- Cuts from June 2024 brought it down to **2.00%** by June 2025.
- Increases on 17 June 2026 (to 2.25%) and 16 September 2026 (to **2.50%**).

The [chart on the indicator page](/indicators/ecb-interest-rates/) draws these steps from the ECB series itself.

## How a decision is made

The Governing Council (the six Executive Board members plus the governors of the euro area national central banks) holds a monetary policy meeting about every six weeks. Governors vote under a monthly rotation system; Executive Board members always vote. The decision is published at **14:15 CET**, the President's press conference starts at **14:45 CET**, and an account of the meeting follows about four weeks later.

The ECB's target is **2% HICP inflation over the medium term**, symmetric, so deviations in both directions are considered equally undesirable. The latest euro area HICP reading on our snapshot was 3.8% for September 2026 (Eurostat); see [HICP vs CPI](/guides/hicp-vs-cpi/) for what that index includes.

## ECB vs Fed: the quick comparison

| | ECB | Federal Reserve |
|---|---|---|
| Policy rate quoted | Deposit facility rate (a single level) | Federal funds target range (25 bp wide) |
| Mandate | Price stability first | Maximum employment and stable prices |
| Inflation measure | HICP | PCE price index |
| Meetings | About every six weeks | Eight scheduled per year |
| Decision time | 14:15 CET | 2:00 p.m. ET |

## How changes reach households

Euro area loans are more often tied to short-term rates than U.S. mortgages, so ECB moves pass through to variable-rate borrowers relatively quickly in countries such as Spain and Italy, typically via Euribor. Deposit rates offered to savers tend to adjust more slowly and by less.

## Bottom line

When someone says "the ECB rate", they almost always mean the deposit facility rate. Check the effective date, compare it with the 2% HICP target, and remember that the other two rates are set relative to it under the post-2024 framework.
